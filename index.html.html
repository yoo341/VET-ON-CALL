Vet-On-Call

Saturday and Sunday animal health care on campus. Book a visit, see what a vet nurse can and cannot do, and check symptoms before you come.

What I can help with

Services are grouped by what a registered veterinary nurse may do on their own, what needs a supervising veterinarian, and what must be referred.

Offered directly

  • Health check and consultationWeight, temperature, gums, coat, eyes, ears, body condition.
  • Vaccination and dewormingFollowing a vet-approved protocol and vaccine schedule.
  • Flea, tick and mite controlApproved over-the-counter products and advice.
  • First aid and wound careCleaning, dressing, bandaging, stabilising before referral.
  • Nursing careFeeding plans, recovery care, monitoring, hygiene, nail and ear care.
  • Sample collectionFaeces, skin scrapings and blood samples for lab testing.
  • Nutrition, husbandry and owner educationDiet, housing, hygiene, zoonosis and rabies awareness.

Only under a veterinarian's direction

  • Giving prescription-only medicinesAntibiotics, injectables and similar, on the vet's written instruction.
  • Fluid therapy and minor proceduresDone to a vet's plan, with the vet reachable.

Referred to a veterinarian

  • Final diagnosis and prescribingNurse findings support, but do not replace, a veterinarian's diagnosis.
  • Surgery, anaesthesia and euthanasiaAlways a veterinarian's decision and procedure.
  • Controlled drugs and health certificatesIssued only by a licensed veterinarian.
  • Suspected notifiable diseaseSuspected rabies, PPR and similar are reported to the veterinary authorities.

Scope of practice: this list follows general veterinary nursing practice. Before publishing it, confirm each item with the Veterinary Council of Ghana and your supervising veterinarian, and check that the campus allows a weekend clinic. Edit the list in the page file if their rules differ.
